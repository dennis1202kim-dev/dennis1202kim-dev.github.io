Seong-Woong Kim

김성웅 박사

Principal Researcher · Extreme Materials Research Institute, KIMS

I develop lightweight, heat-resistant alloys for aircraft and power-generation turbines at the Korea Institute of Materials Science (KIMS). My work examines how lamellar microstructures in γ-TiAl evolve at high temperatures and affect strength and durability, then applies those findings to alloy design, casting, forging, and additive manufacturing.

01 / RECOGNITION

Research Highlights & Awards

TECHNOLOGY HIGHLIGHT

950°C-class TiAl for gas turbines

The team designed an alloy with a thermally stable lamellar microstructure and achieved a uniform structure by vacuum melting, precision casting, and a single heat-treatment step. The alloy demonstrated tensile, creep, and fatigue performance at 950°C, as well as resistance to high-temperature oxidation.

TECHNOLOGY TRANSFER · 2021

Transfer of gas turbine TiAl technology

KIMS transferred its lightweight, heat-resistant TiAl alloy technology to Airgen to support domestic production and industrial adoption of gas turbine materials.

02 / RESEARCH FOCUS

Research Areas

  1. 01

    Alloy design and lamellar microstructures

    Effects of alloying and thermal history on α₂/γ lamella formation, interface migration, and coarsening.

  2. 02

    Casting, forging, and additive manufacturing

    Microstructure and texture control through precision casting, heat treatment, hot forging, and electron-beam and laser powder bed fusion.

  3. 03

    High-temperature strength and durability

    High-temperature tensile, creep, fatigue, and oxidation testing to guide turbine alloy and process development.

  4. 04

    Deformation and fracture mechanisms

    In situ transmission electron microscopy (TEM) of dislocation activity and crack propagation to explain differences in room-temperature ductility and fracture toughness.
